\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}

The study has several limitations. First, UAV-SEAD provides runtime anomaly and fault-domain annotations rather than comprehensive source-module ground truth; consequently, the real-flight experiments cannot validate causal module localization. Second, the document-derived channel-to-subsystem reference is independent of SHAP values but remains author-defined rather than independently expert-adjudicated. Its sensitivity results show that semantic-map agreement is not robust for External or Global Position. Third, no independent uORB gold audit is available: the reported 1.000 value is an internal check sharing the parser's hint vocabulary. The conservative exclusion graph remains a source-path approximation rather than an actual PX4 target-build graph and cannot measure macro-generated, indirect, conditionally compiled, or runtime-created relations. Uniform propagation is also sensitive to topic degree. The version-coherent analysis applies only to the 38.7\% matching-commit anomaly subset and omits runtime activation, scheduling, message rates, parameter-dependent paths, and indirect physical feedback. Fourth, the controlled benchmark contains only three source logs, four mutation types, and 12 fault runs, and the EKF2 case required a generic replay publisher because the source logs lacked \texttt{ekf2\_timestamps}. Two-way cluster-bootstrap intervals remain wide because both cluster dimensions are small. Fifth, the available external datasets support limited validation roles: ALFA does not establish four-domain transfer, and Uncategorized logs have no temporal ranges for point-wise or event-level evaluation. Sixth, recent deep baselines were not fairly retrained at comparable budgets and objectives, so CATCH and the GCAD-inspired ablation remain feasibility references. Finally, the analysis source code and machine-readable Supplement are not public; this reduces independent reproducibility even though the article reports the semantic map, software versions, filters, counts, and statistical settings and selected frozen artifacts can be supplied confidentially.

The explanation analysis also requires class-specific caution. Conservation verifies numerical bookkeeping, while masking supports prediction relevance only under the specified perturbations. External Position agreement remained close to random and Global Position fell below random under the document-derived map. These weaknesses may reflect correlated signals, indirect effects across subsystems, broad diagnostic labels, or attribution limitations, but the present experiments do not isolate them. Consistency@K should therefore remain a sensitivity result rather than a validity criterion.

Future work should expand the present matching-commit analysis into per-commit uORB graphs, obtain independent expert adjudication of the semantic subsystem mapping, extend the parser audit to macro-generated and runtime-created relations, and enlarge the mutation benchmark across independent source logs, PX4 versions, modules, and fault mechanisms, with separate development and test mutations. Detector adaptation could then be studied using only development-domain replay data while preserving untouched real-flight and mutation test sets. Dynamic evidence---including runtime uORB activity, execution traces, and temporal propagation---could complement the static graph, provided that its added information is evaluated against known injected modules. These steps would test whether the current architecture-aware suspect ranking can progress from a useful inspection aid toward reliable detector-gated module diagnosis without overstating causal certainty.
