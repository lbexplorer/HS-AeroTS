\subsection{SHAP Evidence and Software-Module Suspect Ranking}

The explanation analysis was conducted on the 3602 anomalous test windows from the fixed chronological protocol. For each window, the TreeSHAP contributions of the predicted Stage 2 class were converted to absolute evidence and aggregated from statistical features to telemetry channels, PX4 topics, and subsystems. The aggregation preserved the total evidence mass: the maximum absolute conservation error across the hierarchy was $7.11 \times 10^{-15}$. This result verifies the numerical redistribution of the evidence, but it does not establish that the attributed channel or subsystem is the physical source of the anomaly.

The masking analysis tested whether the validation-derived SHAP ranking was more informative than an equal-size random feature selection. The top-$k$ SHAP features were ranked on validation anomaly windows and replaced in the test data by feature-wise medians calculated from the training windows. The Macro-F1 decrease for SHAP masking increased from $0.0409 \pm 0.0039$ at $k=10$ to $0.2683 \pm 0.0343$ at $k=100$. The corresponding decreases for uniform random masking were $0.0005 \pm 0.0020$ and $0.0037 \pm 0.0080$, respectively. The SHAP-ranked features therefore produced a larger prediction degradation at all evaluated masking sizes, with the largest separation at $k=100$. This is evidence for prediction-relevant feature concentration under the tested masking protocol, rather than evidence of causal influence.

At the telemetry-channel level, the overall Consistency@1 was $0.3705 \pm 0.0109$, compared with the exact random baseline of 0.1703. Across $K \in \{1,3,5,10\}$, Consistency@K was $0.3705 \pm 0.0109$, $0.3741 \pm 0.0175$, $0.3538 \pm 0.0061$, and $0.2999 \pm 0.0028$, respectively. The corresponding Hit@K values increased from $0.3705 \pm 0.0109$ to $0.8550 \pm 0.0167$ as $K$ increased. Class-level Consistency@1 was heterogeneous: the means were 0.0363 for External Position, 0.8452 for Global Position, 0.2287 for Altitude, and 0.8788 for Mechanical/Electrical. The External Position value was close to its class-specific random baseline of 0.0345, whereas the other classes exceeded their respective random baselines. This near-random External Position result is an explicit limitation of the evidence consistency analysis.

For the architecture-aware layer, the firmware audit identified seven commits, and the most frequent commit occurred in 562 logs. The static scan was matched to commit \texttt{82aa24adfca29321cfd1209e287eab6c2b16780e}. All 18 SHAP topics had at least one publisher, 16 had at least one subscriber, and the resulting mapping contained 180 topic-role-module edges covering 54 PX4 modules. Under the aggregate producer-only propagation mode, the highest-ranked module suspects were \texttt{src/modules/ekf2} (SHAP share 0.2484), \texttt{src/modules/mavlink} (0.2095), \texttt{src/modules/position\_estimator\_inav} (0.0896), \texttt{src/modules/sensors} (0.0792), and \texttt{src/modules/vtol\_att\_control} (0.0455). These are static, version-dependent suspect rankings derived from topic evidence. They are not causal root-cause labels or end-to-end software fault-localization results.
