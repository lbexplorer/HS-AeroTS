\subsection{PX4 Architecture-Aware Diagnostic Evidence}

PX4 organizes flight-control functionality as interacting modules rather than as a monolithic program. Its uORB middleware provides typed publish--subscribe communication through which estimators, controllers, sensor drivers, and other services exchange state and command data \cite{meier2015px4,px4uorbdocs2026}. Because the same topic vocabulary appears in ULog telemetry, uORB provides a practical bridge between an influential logged channel and the software components that publish or consume its corresponding topic. This bridge supports architecture-aware inspection when firmware provenance is retained: module boundaries, topic definitions, and communication relations can change across PX4 revisions, so the source tree and topic graph must be tied to a specified revision.

The uORB graph supports several complementary interpretations of diagnostic evidence. A producer-oriented view emphasizes modules that may have generated an abnormal value; a consumer-oriented view emphasizes modules whose behavior may have been affected by that value; and a bidirectional view retains both roles when the available evidence does not justify choosing between them. Propagating topic-level importance over these relations can reduce a large source tree to a reviewable set of software-module suspects. The relationship to HS-AeroTS is therefore architectural and methodological: SHAP supplies topic-level evidence, while the uORB graph supplies a version-specific relation over which that evidence can be propagated. Subscription does not establish that an execution path was active, publication does not establish that the produced value was erroneous, and high-connectivity topics may implicate many modules; the resulting list is consequently an inspection aid rather than a causal diagnosis.

This setting is related to, but distinct from, conventional software fault localization. Spectrum-based approaches rank statements, methods, or files by contrasting their execution in passing and failing tests; early visualization work demonstrated how such suspiciousness information can guide inspection, and subsequent surveys systematized a broad family of statistical ranking techniques \cite{jones2002visualization,wong2016survey}. Their evaluation normally assumes a known faulty entity and reports measures such as Top-$k$ accuracy, mean reciprocal rank, or inspection effort. Studies using seeded faults and real defects have also shown that conclusions can depend on the benchmark, fault population, and evaluation protocol \cite{pearson2017evaluating,papadakis2019mutation}. HS-AeroTS uses controlled PX4 source mutations when source-level ground truth is required, while real-flight telemetry supports runtime and fault-domain evidence rather than verified defective modules.

Accordingly, the present work positions commit-specific topic-to-module propagation as an architecture-aware diagnostic evidence layer, not as causal root-cause analysis. Runtime anomaly labels support evaluation of anomaly detection and fault-domain diagnosis, whereas controlled replay with known source mutations evaluates software-module suspect ranking against a source-level target. In that setting, both detection specificity and candidate ranking require evaluation: a high rank does not establish that the run was correctly distinguished from a healthy control. The resulting list is a candidate representation conditional on the observed telemetry, its evidence source, and the reconstructed PX4 revision. Flights from other revisions require their own mapping or an explicit compatibility analysis.

Across these three research areas, HS-AeroTS is positioned as an auditable integration rather than a replacement for the underlying methods. UAV telemetry research supplies the runtime detection and fault-domain foundation; explainability research supplies attribution and perturbation-based evaluation; and PX4/uORB research supplies the architecture-specific relation needed to express topic evidence as software-module inspection candidates. The contribution of the present work lies in connecting these evidence levels under explicit protocols while preserving their different ground truths and capability boundaries.
